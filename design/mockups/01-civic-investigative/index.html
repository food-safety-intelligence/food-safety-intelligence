Which Chicago kitchens
are trending toward trouble?

Every weekday, Chicago's Department of Public Health publishes inspection results going back to 2010. We pair that record with 311 sanitation reports and license history to estimate the chance any given restaurant will receive a failed inspection or a priority violation in the next 180 days. The result is a single, explainable score — and the reasons it is what it is.

Editor's note

A risk signal, not a verdict.

A high score does not mean a restaurant is unsafe today, nor that it will fail an inspection. It means the public record suggests elevated risk of a failed inspection or priority violation sometime in the next six months. New restaurants with no prior record will always look lower-risk than ones with a long track record. We expose why a score is what it is so you can decide what to do with it.
